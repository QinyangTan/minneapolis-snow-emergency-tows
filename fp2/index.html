Coverage & denominators

The original nine-event baseline contains 5,423 records; 5,355 map to 87 neighborhoods and 68 do not. This is a selected sample. Zero means no record in this sample, not no towing ever. Population scaling uses 2020–2024 ACS residents, not parked vehicles or daily visitors. 86 neighborhood names match the city ACS extracts; Humboldt Industrial Area has no matched estimate.

Income, housing & uncertainty

Economic indicators use ACS 2019–2023, while housing and population use ACS 2020–2024. They are overlapping five-year estimates, not event-time measurements. Source margins of error and reliability appear in the neighborhood detail; Spearman ρ does not propagate this uncertainty or adjust for spatial dependence. Per-capita income is shown at its native census tract scale in the context view; allocating it to neighborhoods is deferred.

Sale medians use 1,472 qualifying transactions from 2024; 54 neighborhoods have at least 10. They reflect the mix of homes sold, not every home's value. Missing or suppressed indicators are excluded from the scatterplot, never zero-filled.

Why no ordinary-day comparison yet?

A complete public Minneapolis actual-towing log with reason codes was not verified. 311 requests, 911 calls, citations and crashes are not completed tows. Citywide annual category totals cannot supply neighborhood ordinary-day counts, so that comparison is deferred. Traffic and per-capita income are available in the additional context view. Transit, weather and parking archives are saved for further event-aligned analysis.

FP2 narrative & next validation

The three steps above move from geographic concentration to population adjustment and then contextual association. Selection persists across coordinated views; event/day changes test whether a pattern is stable. The next stage can animate transitions between the same views while preserving scales and respecting reduced-motion preferences.

Before a stronger claim: audit baseline source coverage, align event-time parking exposure and traffic, check spatial boundaries, propagate ACS uncertainty, and obtain actual towing reasons. Peer feedback: does population adjustment help? Which parking-exposure denominator is defensible? Are sparse or uncertain estimates clear?
